What canned beverage co-packers need to know

Can size, line style, carbonation level, formula characteristics and expected run volume can change how a facility assesses the project. Include your preferred can format, desired unit count and whether you have printed cans or another decoration method.

Co-packing versus full co-manufacturing

Co-packing may mean filling an already-developed formula. Co-manufacturing can involve a broader scope, including scale-up, sourcing, production coordination and packaging. Define what you need the partner to own.

Plan for the full finished-goods unit

The cost and timeline extend beyond liquid filling. Tray, carton, case, pallet pattern and other components can influence the scope. Include distribution plans and whether you want individual units, four-packs or other sellable configurations.

Keep flexibility in the production plan

Equipment configuration and scheduling are specific to each facility. We help turn your preferred scenario into questions that a prospective co-packer can answer before you place component orders.

Common questions

Some operators have multiple lines; others specialize. We assess fit based on your requirements.

Not necessarily. Decoration strategies depend on your volumes, branding and facility capabilities.

No. Technical specifications are set and confirmed through the partner's product-development and manufacturing process.
