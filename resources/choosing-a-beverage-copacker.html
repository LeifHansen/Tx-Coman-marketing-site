How to Choose a Beverage Co-Packer in Texas

Before you compare quotes, make sure the facilities are quoting the same project.

Published November 18, 2026 · Commercial planning resource

The cheapest line item on a co-packing estimate is not automatically the lowest-cost production partner. A strong decision compares format compatibility, formula readiness, responsibilities, finished-goods scope and whether the manufacturer can support your operational stage.

1. Start with a complete product description

List the intended beverage type, liquid characteristics, package material, container size, whether the product is still or sparkling, and any specialty ingredients. Without those details, initial quotes tend to be so conditional that they are hard to compare.

2. Identify what the manufacturer is responsible for

There is a meaningful difference between filling an established formula supplied by the brand and building a commercial process around a new concept. Clarify who sources ingredients and packaging, owns development work, orders testing, handles finished goods and organizes freight.

3. Ask what the filling line actually handles

Determine which can or bottle sizes are supported, how closures and labels are applied, whether secondary packaging is included and how date coding is managed. Do not assume that a facility offering 'beverage co-packing' supports every SKU type.

4. Compare total landed scope

Break quotes into formulation or transfer, ingredients, liquid processing, filling, containers, labels or sleeves, cartons, quality activities, storage and logistics. Confirm how line setup costs, waste allowances and volume tiers are handled.

5. Understand project risk and scheduling

Ask about ingredient lead times, approval steps, packaging purchase deadlines and what happens if a run is delayed. Get the agreed specifications and responsibilities documented before money is committed.

6. Use partner fit as the decision criterion

A smaller facility may align better with a development-stage concept; another operation may fit repeat high-volume production. Your best choice depends on what stage you are at and what the next run needs to accomplish.

Quick project checklist

  • Product description and target customer
  • Package type, size and decoration
  • Formula status and existing technical documents
  • Ingredient sourcing and production responsibilities
  • First-run and annual unit forecasts
  • Target production and distribution timing
  • Finished-goods packaging requirements
  • Budget range and approval process
