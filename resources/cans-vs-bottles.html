FORMAT DECISION GUIDE

Cans vs. Bottles for Hemp THC and CBD Beverages

Packaging affects the line, the formula, the shelf experience and the economics—not just the label design.

Published November 18, 2026 · Commercial planning resource

A brand's package is part of the commercial production specification. Cans, glass and PET each create different choices around handling, component procurement, decoration, freight and the desired customer experience. Treat the decision as part of manufacturing planning.

Aluminum cans: ready-to-drink simplicity

Cans are familiar to beverage buyers and work well for many sparkling RTD concepts. Key planning points include standard versus specialty dimensions, printed cans or applied decoration, carbonation process compatibility, secondary packaging and finished-goods configuration.

Glass bottles: premium presentation and tradeoffs

Glass can support premium mixers, tonics and larger shareable presentations. It also introduces breakage, weight and closure considerations. Check the desired bottle shape and neck finish against the exact bottling line before purchasing components.

PET bottles: lightweight but process-specific

PET can provide weight advantages and practical versatility, but suitability depends on the formula, intended handling, process and shelf-life targets. Technical evaluation is still essential.

Which format fits a THC or CBD beverage?

Start with the liquid: still or sparkling, single-serve or shareable, ready-to-drink or concentrate, existing commercial recipe or concept. Then assess required equipment, packaging costs, desired retail price and distribution handling.

Avoid ordering components before confirmation

A bottle or can that works in a mockup may not fit the selected line. Match the technical spec with the actual manufacturer before committing to significant printed packaging quantities.

Ask for a like-for-like production quote

Quotes should specify container dimensions, fill volume, decoration, secondary packaging, finished-goods counts and the responsibilities included. Without that consistency, format comparisons can be misleading.

Quick project checklist

  • Desired serving and presentation
  • Still or sparkling formula
  • Intended sales channels and shipping
  • Preferred material and closure
  • Available production volume
  • Packaging vendor and component lead times
